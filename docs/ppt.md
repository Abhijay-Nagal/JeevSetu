# The Challenge We're Addressing

BNHS's digital presence is rich but fragmented — Hornbill magazine, newsletters,
the website & Virtual Museum, the Birdle game, the Jalchar app, and citizen-science
channels like Report a Tagged Bird all exist, but as disconnected silos.

- New online visitors don't know where to start or how these pieces connect to BNHS's mission
- People browse without progressing to real actions — subscribing, joining, reporting, using existing tools
- No path exists today for someone who will *never* visit a BNHS centre or event
- **The ask:** a standalone digital entry point that redirects to BNHS's *existing* channels — not a replacement, and explicitly not another flora/fauna photo-ID app (BNHS doesn't want to compete with eBird/iNaturalist) — that converts curiosity into participation **without adding operational overhead** for BNHS staff

---

# Our Solution — JeevSetu

One digital hub that organizes what BNHS already has and turns visitors into
returning, contributing advocates — entirely through existing channels, with
no new workload for BNHS staff.

- **Knowledge Hub** — AI search & chatbot grounded in BNHS's real archive: Hornbill editions, JBNHS journal, field guides, species records, blog posts, and newsletters — with cited sources, not fabricated answers
- **Guided next steps** — every search/chat/quiz ends by pointing users at a real BNHS action: membership, a nature camp, the shop, or the newsletter
- **Community** — users post observations, like and comment on each other's finds — conservation becomes social, not a one-way form
- **Daily engagement loop** — a daily quiz question, streaks, and coins turn a one-time visit into a returning habit
- **Publications** — a lightweight digital reporting channel for research findings, echoing bands@bnhs.org, with an automatic check against BNHS's existing records first
- BNHS's role stays exactly what it is today — reviewing submissions from a table — nothing new to operate or maintain

---

# Who It's For

Matches the brief's stakeholder universe directly: youth, non-local, diverse
online users who need a reason to stay engaged.

- **Curious first-timers** who land on BNHS content with no map of where to go next
- **Students & citizen scientists** who want to learn and contribute without visiting in person
- **Birdwatchers & nature enthusiasts** looking for a community to share sightings with
- **Research-minded users** who've found something worth reporting but have no clear digital channel
- **Existing BNHS supporters** who want a reason to keep returning, not just a one-time visit

---

# Key Ideas

- **Standalone, not competing** — no photo-ID/species-identification feature; we organize and redirect, we don't replace eBird/iNaturalist-style tools
- **Zero new operational overhead** — every "solution" is either AI-generated from BNHS's own public data or a database row BNHS already knows how to query
- **Grounded, not generic** — retrieval-augmented answers cited to BNHS's real Hornbill/JBNHS/newsletter archive, so nothing is made up
- **Passive → active** — gamified daily habit (streaks, coins) converts browsing into a returning habit
- **Every interaction has a next step** — search, chat, and quizzes all end pointing at a real BNHS channel, never a dead end
- **Advocates, not just visitors** — community + rewards + a real reporting channel turn a one-time click into ongoing participation
